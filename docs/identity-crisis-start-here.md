# Start here

You're about to build a brand identity for your coaching business. Not a logo — the part underneath that decides what the logo should be.

It takes **60 to 90 minutes** and it works best in one sitting. You'll be answering questions, not reading. Have somewhere to think out loud.

---

## What you need

An AI account. Either works:

- **Claude** — claude.ai
- **ChatGPT** — chatgpt.com

A paid account is better than a free one, mostly because you won't hit a limit mid-session. Not required.

## What to do

**If you were sent a file** (a `.md` file, or a big wall of text):

1. Open a new chat.
2. Paste the whole thing in. It's long. That's fine — paste all of it.
3. Send this: **"Run Identity Crisis on my coaching business."**

That's it. It'll start asking you questions.

**If your AI has "Projects" or "Custom GPTs"** — better option, worth the extra two minutes. Make a new project, paste the file into the project's instructions or knowledge, then start a chat inside it. It stays loaded that way, so you can come back tomorrow and pick up where you left off.

**If you use Claude and were sent a `.skill` file** — open the file and Claude will offer to save it. Then just say "Run Identity Crisis on my coaching business" in any chat.

---

## Before you start, get these together

You don't need all of it. The more you have, the better this goes — because the honest material is in what you've already got, not in what you'd say about yourself if asked cold.

- **Every certification and training you use with clients.** All of them, even the ones you never mention.
- **Your website, Instagram, LinkedIn** — whatever's public.
- **Three to five testimonials or reviews.** Don't clean them up. The typos are the point.
- **The stuff that didn't work.** A program you launched that flopped, a pivot you abandoned, a marketing push that went nowhere. This turns out to be the single most useful thing you bring. It's uncomfortable and it's worth it.
- **Three things you've written that you almost liked.** Not your best — your near-misses.
- **Rough numbers.** How many clients or members, what you charge, how long people stay.
- **Any recordings or transcripts** of sessions or calls, if you have them and have permission to use them.

## What you'll walk out with

A **Brand Bible** — a written document covering:

- What you're against, and what you stand for
- Who you are in this work, and how that shapes everything else
- The expensive problem you solve and the change you actually produce
- Two to four ideal-client profiles, built as *ways people get stuck* rather than as demographics
- Your vocabulary — the words you own, and a banned list of words that get a draft thrown out
- How you sound in content, in sales, and in the room, which are three different volumes of the same voice
- Fill-in-the-blank patterns so you (or a writer, or an AI) can produce copy that sounds like you
- A quality bar to check anything against before it ships

You can hand it to a copywriter, a designer, a VA, or an AI tool and get back something that sounds like you.

**What you won't get:** colors, fonts, a logo. That's a separate session, on purpose. Visual work done before the words are settled gets redone.

---

## Four things worth knowing before you start

**It asks what you're against before it asks who you are.** This feels backwards and negative. Stay with it. Almost nobody can answer "who are you as a coach" — everybody can answer "what's the lie in my industry," and the second question gets you to the first one. If you skip this part you'll end up with the same brand as everyone else.

**It'll push back on your answers.** When you say something, it may argue the opposite at you. That's deliberate — it's how you avoid ending up with the obvious version that four thousand other coaches already have. You don't have to take the opposite. You do have to look at it.

**Near the end it will try to tear the whole thing apart.** It'll list everything wrong with what you just built. This is the most valuable part of the session and it's the part people want to skip. An AI that helped you build something will not spontaneously attack it, so it's been told to. Let it.

**The last step is homework with real humans.** Five conversations with real people who fit your ideal client — not to sell them, to listen. Everything before that is a hypothesis. This is the step that tells you whether you built something true or something that merely sounds good.

---

## If it starts writing things you wouldn't say

Stop it and say so. "That doesn't sound like me." "I'd never use that word." "Say that the way I said it."

The whole thing only works if it ends up in your voice. A document that's better written than anything you've ever said is a document you'll quietly stop using. Push back early and often — it's built to expect that.
