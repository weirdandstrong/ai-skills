# The Stress Test

Phase 6. Two halves — an adversarial review, then falsifiable homework. Both are required.

The reason this phase exists: everything up to now has been a conversation between a coach and a machine that is structurally inclined to agree with them. The coach is going to feel great. Feeling great is not evidence, and the worst outcome this kit can produce is a beautifully coherent brand that the market has never been asked about.

---

## Half A · The Hard Cuts

### How to run it

Deliver the cuts **one at a time**, not as a list. A list gets skimmed and the coach accepts the easy ones. One at a time, each gets a real answer.

For each cut: state the charge, show the specific evidence from what you've built together, and ask for a verdict — **accept, reject, or defer.**

Then honor the verdict. A rejected cut is information: it tells you what the coach actually believes and won't be talked out of, and that conviction is usually load-bearing. Record rejections in the Bible. Don't relitigate.

**Do not soften a cut because the coach is excited.** Their excitement is the thing you're testing. If a charge is real, say it plainly and let them decide.

**Tone:** you are on their side. This is not a takedown — it's the last cheap chance to find the flaw. Frame it that way and coaches engage with it rather than defending.

---

### The standing charges

Run all ten. Add the lens-specific charges from the relevant lens file.

**1 · Aesthetic differentiation masquerading as categorical differentiation.**
A distinctive palette, a coined vocabulary, and a strong voice can make an ordinary offer feel novel. Strip the words and the design: what is structurally different about what this client receives, compared to the three closest alternatives? If the answer is "the feel of it," that's a real advantage but a fragile one, and the coach should know which they've got.

**2 · Marketing voice versus delivery voice.**
Read a piece of the content voice and a piece of the in-session voice back to back. Would a client meeting both feel misled? A combative brand attached to a gentle program produces refunds in week two. Either the delivery gets sharper or the marketing gets honest — but they have to agree.

**3 · Too many personas.**
Which single one is the beachhead for the next two quarters? Four personas is a research finding; it is not a plan. If the coach can't pick, ask which one has already paid them, and start there.

**4 · Price without proof.**
What evidence supports this number? Conviction is not evidence. If there are no results yet, that's fine — but the pricing story needs to be about the cost of inaction and the access being purchased, not about outcomes that haven't happened. Also check the reverse: a price far below the value described is its own credibility problem.

**5 · Positioning that self-destructs on success.**
Some positions depend on being the outsider, the small one, the one nobody's heard of, or the one attacking the establishment. Run the brand at a hundred times its current size and see what breaks. If success makes the coach into the thing they're against, revise now.

**6 · Scope, safety, and the person in crisis.**
Someone is going to arrive who needs more than coaching. What happens? This is not paperwork — it's the exposure that ends coaching businesses and hurts people. Required: a screening question, a referral list, a clear scope statement, and a rule for what the coach does when they see something clinical. Any brand that trades in psychological language needs this before it takes money.

**7 · The brand as a burnout script for the coach.**
Especially, but not only, when the brand is *about* burnout. Does this identity require constant output, constant sharpness, constant visibility? What does it look like at 60% capacity? If there's no answer, the coach has designed a job they'll quit.

**8 · Collision with existing obligations.**
Day job, non-compete, current clients, a partner's expectations, a landlord, a licensing body. Does anything here create a conflict the coach hasn't looked at directly? Ask about the day job specifically — it's the most commonly avoided answer in the whole phase.

**9 · Machinery before humans.**
Is the coach about to build a funnel, an email sequence, a course platform, and a booking system before a single person has paid them for this? Sequence it: sell to five people by hand first. Automation of a thing that doesn't convert just makes the failure faster and more expensive.

**10 · Unresolved contradictions.**
Every collision the Contradiction Check surfaced along the way, gathered and stated once more together. Existing name versus new positioning. Program names versus the archetype. Public bio versus the identity. The coach can keep all of it — but it goes in the Bible as a known, chosen contradiction rather than an accident.

---

### After the cuts

Revise whatever the coach accepted, then run the **Vocational-Fit Check** one final time on the revised version:

- Could you make this argument, in this voice, every week for a year — and still want to?
- Is this pointed at what you're actually good at?
- Reading it now, after all that: does it make you want to go post something, or does it make you tired?

If the energy dropped during the cuts, find out where. Sometimes a cut removed something that was doing real work and it needs restoring in a different form. Sometimes the drop reveals the coach was in love with a version of themselves rather than the work, which is worth knowing before they spend a year on it.

## Half B · The Field Test

The homework. Without it, everything above is a hall of mirrors.

### The assignment, as you should give it to the coach

> Find five real people in your network who fit your primary persona. Not clients — people. A former colleague, a friend of a friend, someone in a group you're in.
>
> Ask each of them for thirty minutes. Tell them the truth: you're doing research and you want to understand their experience, not sell them anything. **Do not pitch. Do not describe your brand.** The moment they know what you want to hear, the data is gone.
>
> Ask about their experience of the problem: when it started, what they've tried, what it's cost them, what they've spent money on, what they'd say to someone else in it.
>
> Record them, with permission. Bring the recordings back.

### Why the no-pitch rule is absolute

The coach has just spent hours building a beautiful description of this person's inner life. They will be desperate to test it, and testing it by describing it is how you get a false positive — people agree with confident descriptions of themselves, especially from someone they like.

The only useful signal is unprompted language: whether they reach for the same words the personas use, whether the expensive problem shows up in their account of their own life without being suggested, and whether the false cures on the persona's spend list appear on theirs.

### Interview questions to hand the coach

- Tell me about the last time [the problem] really got in the way. What happened?
- How long has this been going on?
- What have you tried? Walk me through each one.
- What did you think each of those was going to do? What actually happened?
- What has this cost you? *(Then stay quiet. The silence does the work.)*
- If nothing changes in two years, where are you?
- What would have to be true for you to spend real money solving this?
- Who else do you know dealing with the same thing?

### What to do with the recordings

Hand off to the **Persona Validation** skill. It compares real language against the drafted personas and reports where they hold, where they drift, and where the coach invented something that doesn't exist.

Until that's done, mark the personas in the Bible as **UNVALIDATED** in plain sight. A coach who forgets which parts are hypotheses will build a year of content on a person who isn't real.

---

## What good looks like coming out of this phase

- Three to six cuts accepted and the brand revised for them.
- One or two cuts rejected, with the coach's reasoning recorded.
- A named beachhead persona.
- A scope-and-referral answer that exists in writing.
- Five names on a list, with the field-test assignment scheduled.
- The coach still wanting to do it. If that's gone, something upstream is wrong and it's worth going back rather than shipping a Bible into a shrug.
